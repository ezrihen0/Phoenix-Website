## Executive Summary

WETT Inc. describes SITE® (System Inspections and Technical Evaluation) as its standardized inspection/evaluation process for solid-fuel-burning systems. The current public WETT material identifies three inspection levels:

1. **Visual Inspection — Readily Accessible**
2. **Technical Inspection — Accessible**
3. **Invasive Inspection — Concealed Accessibility**

The inspection level controls how far the inspector is expected to go to access components. A lower-level inspection is not a promise that every part of the system has been inspected. WETT reporting expressly allows items to be recorded as **Unable to Inspect (UTI)** where the agreed inspection level does not permit or does not achieve access.

Current WETT public material states that every inspection is documented using WETT inspection forms and that each applicable report section is classified as one of:

- **Compliant**
- **Not Compliant**
- **Not Applicable**
- **Unable to Inspect (UTI)**

A WETT-certified person does **not** “certify” an appliance, installation, company, or system. WETT certification applies to individuals. The deliverable is an **inspection report**, not a “WETT certificate.”

A WETT inspection is a point-in-time assessment. WETT states that an inspection report records what was observed at the date and time of inspection; it cannot guarantee that the system remains unchanged afterward. Insurer acceptance periods are determined by the insurer, not by WETT.

The strongest operational principle for this knowledge program is:

**Observed fact → inspection item → access level → applicable authority → report status → professional wording.**

# Verified Knowledge

## 1. What a WETT Inspection™ Is

### Classification
**WETT REQUIREMENT / PROCEDURE**

### Verified rule

WETT describes a WETT Inspection™ as an inspection/evaluation of a solid-fuel-burning system performed by a WETT-certified professional for compliance with applicable codes, standards, and manufacturer instructions.

The inspection is not a WETT “approval” of the appliance or installation.

### Critical terminology

Incorrect customer language commonly includes:

- “WETT certificate”
- “WETT certified fireplace”
- “WETT certified installation”
- “WETT certified company”

The technically correct deliverable is:

**A WETT inspection report completed by a WETT-certified individual.**

### Inspector application

The report should identify the WETT-certified individual and include the member’s WETT certification number.

### Reporting guidance

Preferred customer-facing wording:

> A WETT inspection report documents the condition and compliance findings observed within the agreed inspection scope. WETT certification applies to the inspector, not to the appliance or installation itself.

## 2. SITE® — System Inspections and Technical Evaluation

### Classification
**WETT REQUIREMENT / PROCEDURE**

### Verified rule

SITE® is WETT’s standardized inspection/evaluation framework for solid-fuel-burning systems.

Current WETT material divides SITE® inspections into three levels:

| Level | WETT Name | Accessibility Concept |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Visual Inspection | Readily Accessible |
| 2 | Technical Inspection | Accessible |
| 3 | Invasive Inspection | Concealed Accessibility |

The level should be selected according to the client’s need, system condition, known concerns, and the amount of access required.

An inspection may include **more** than the minimum scope of the selected level, but WETT’s SITE material states that it should not include **less** than the defined minimum for that level.

# 3. Level 1 — Visual Inspection

## Title
Visual Inspection — Readily Accessible

**Jurisdiction:** Canada — WETT common methodology  
**Business:** BOTH  
**System:** Solid-fuel-burning appliance and venting system  
**Inspection Level:** Visual  
**Classification:** WETT REQUIREMENT / PROCEDURE

### Requirement

A Visual inspection is a non-invasive inspection of visible/readily accessible system components.

WETT defines **Readily Accessible** as something that can be quickly or easily reached for inspection.

Public WETT guidance states that a Visual inspection does not require special tools to open/remove panels, doors, coverings, or system components. Ladder use is not normally required unless the inspector and client elect otherwise.

### Typical reasons for use

WETT identifies Visual inspections as commonly relevant for:

- Real-estate transactions.
- Insurance requests.
- Authority Having Jurisdiction requirements.
- Fire Code-related requests.
- Personal peace of mind before use.

This does **not** mean every insurer, real-estate transaction, or jurisdiction requires the same level. The requesting party may set additional requirements.

### What the Inspector Checks

Verified public WETT scope includes:

- Visible solid-fuel appliance components.
- Visible venting-system components.
- Required clearances that are within the Visual inspection scope.
- Stove doors.
- Ground-accessible dampers.
- Ground-accessible clean-out doors.
- Chimney visually from the ground.
- Easily visible portions of the flue.
- Visible deficiencies.
- Red flags indicating need for more detailed inspection.

### Measurements Required

At minimum, WETT public material specifically identifies **clearance measurements** as part of the Visual inspection.

The exact clearance requirement is **not established in this phase**. It must later be traced to the applicable code, standard, listing, or manufacturer instructions.

### Typical Tools

WETT public material identifies basic tools such as:

- Measuring tape.
- Flashlight.
- Mirror.
- Camera.
- Selfie stick and/or other basic viewing tools may also be used.

### Outside the Normal Visual Scope

The public SITE table identifies the following as outside the ordinary Visual scope:

- Disassembling flue-pipe assemblies or other components.
- Opening dampers that are not readily accessible.
- Clean-outs requiring tools for access.
- Roof access by ladder as a required element.
- Attic access other than readily accessible/walk-up access.
- Remedial work.
- Flue portions that are not readily/easily visible.

### UTI Considerations

If a report item cannot be inspected within the agreed Visual scope, it may need to be classified **Unable to Inspect (UTI)** rather than assumed compliant or deficient.

A Visual inspection therefore does **not** support claims about concealed or otherwise inaccessible system areas.

### Escalation Trigger

A Technical inspection may be recommended where:

- A Visual inspection identifies signs of concern.
- A component needs hands-on access to evaluate.
- Important areas cannot be assessed under Visual access limitations.
- The system has been unused for a significant period.
- Components have been modified/replaced and more detailed review is appropriate.
- The insurer or Authority Having Jurisdiction requests a Technical inspection.

### Photo / Evidence Required

WETT member-facing SITE material states that the report should include the **mandatory photos required by the WETT Inspection SOP**.

However, the exact current mandatory-photo list was not located in accessible public material during this phase.

**Verification Status: PARTIALLY VERIFIED**

### Report Wording

Example:

> Visual inspection completed within the readily accessible scope of SITE®. Components that could not be inspected within this level are identified as Unable to Inspect where applicable. No conclusion is made regarding concealed or inaccessible portions of the system.

# 4. Level 2 — Technical Inspection

## Title
Technical Inspection — Accessible

**Jurisdiction:** Canada — WETT common methodology  
**Business:** BOTH  
**System:** Solid-fuel-burning appliance and venting system  
**Inspection Level:** Technical  
**Classification:** WETT REQUIREMENT / PROCEDURE

### Requirement

A Technical inspection includes all Visual-inspection elements plus hands-on access to components that are **Accessible** without invasive building work.

WETT defines Accessible as able to be reached for inspection **without invasive methods** being used to access the building, property, or system components.

### What the Inspector Checks

In addition to Visual inspection items, Technical inspection may include:

- Moving/removing doors.
- Moving/removing panels or coverings.
- Taking apart flue pipes.
- Opening clean-outs.
- Entering an attic.
- Accessing the chimney from the roof.
- Reviewing the condition of exposed/removed components.
- Observing creosote quantity where visible in removed/exposed components or chimney sections.
- Recording where access was achieved and where it was not.

### Accessibility Boundary

A defining characteristic of Technical access is that accessed components can generally be repositioned to their original location/condition.

Opening walls, ceilings, or similar invasive building work is outside normal Technical scope.

### Tools

In addition to Visual-level tools:

- Ladder(s).
- Screwdrivers.
- Wrenches.
- Pliers.
- Other hand tools appropriate to the inspection.

### Chimney Cleaning / Liner Visibility

WETT’s member SITE table notes that a chimney may need sweeping before or after the Technical inspection to allow inspection of the liner, depending on circumstances.

The inspection itself should not silently be treated as a chimney sweeping service unless sweeping is separately within the agreed work.

### Defect Integrity Warning

The member SITE table states that if defects compromising component integrity are found during Technical inspection, affected components may not be appropriate for reassembly until corrective action has been taken, and the client should be notified in writing not to use the appliance until corrected.

Because the exact application depends on the defect and inspector judgment, operational use must remain tied to the actual observed condition.

### UTI Considerations

Technical inspection does not eliminate UTI.

If components remain inaccessible without invasive methods, those areas may remain UTI and escalation to Invasive inspection may be appropriate.

### Escalation Trigger

Invasive inspection may be appropriate where:

- Concealed components require investigation.
- A chimney fire has occurred or is suspected.
- A seismic/structural incident creates concern.
- A serious concern cannot be resolved through Technical access.
- The insurer or Authority Having Jurisdiction requests it.

### Follow-Up From Visual Inspection

Where a Technical inspection follows a Visual inspection:

- The prior report should be made available by the client.
- WETT member material states that a **full new WETT inspection form** should be completed with additional notes.
- Areas that were UTI during the earlier inspection may become reportable if Technical access resolves them.

### Photo / Evidence Required

WETT member-facing SITE material again refers to mandatory photos required by the WETT Inspection SOP.

Exact current mandatory-photo schedule:

### Report Wording

Example:

> Technical inspection completed within the accessible SITE® scope. Components accessed through hand-tool removal, attic/roof access, or disassembly are documented in this report. Components requiring invasive building work remain outside this inspection level and are identified accordingly.

# 5. Level 3 — Invasive Inspection

## Title
Invasive Inspection — Concealed Accessibility

**Jurisdiction:** Canada — WETT common methodology  
**Business:** BOTH  
**System:** Solid-fuel-burning appliance and venting system  
**Inspection Level:** Invasive  
**Classification:** WETT REQUIREMENT / PROCEDURE

### Requirement

An Invasive inspection is used when evaluation requires access to concealed components that cannot be reached through Visual or Technical methods.

WETT defines **Concealed Accessibility** as areas not accessible except through invasive methods.

### What the Inspector May Access

Public WETT material identifies activities including:

- Opening walls.
- Opening ceilings.
- Invasive/disassembly work on masonry chimneys.
- Invasive/disassembly work on prefabricated chimneys.
- Examination of chimney liners.
- Use of specialty inspection equipment.
- Video scanning where appropriate.
- Use of power tools such as drills or saws, based on the work required.

### Restoration Limitation

WETT public guidance notes that property/system components removed during an Invasive inspection may not be capable of being restored to their original condition as part of the inspection itself.

Repair/reconstruction is not automatically included in the inspection.

### Client Agreement

WETT public material states that the inspector should obtain written agreement from the client before invasive methods are used.

### Permit Boundary

WETT public consumer guidance states that a permit would be required from the Authority Having Jurisdiction when structural building components are compromised.

The actual permit process is jurisdiction-specific and therefore belongs in the Ontario/Alberta/municipal layers.

### Typical Reasons for Invasive Inspection

- Confirmed or suspected chimney fire.
- Structural/seismic incident.
- Serious suspected system damage.
- Concealed components requiring investigation.
- Technical inspection unable to resolve critical concerns.
- Insurance or AHJ-directed investigation.

### Reporting

A final WETT inspection report should document findings and include narrative explanations describing the invasive work and results.

### Report Wording

Example:

> Invasive inspection was performed to evaluate concealed components not assessable through Visual or Technical access. Areas opened, components examined, and any limitations are documented in this report. Repair or restoration work is outside the inspection scope unless separately contracted.

## Unable to Inspect (UTI)

### Verified principle
WETT reports use **Unable to Inspect (UTI)** for items that cannot be inspected within the circumstances and agreed inspection level.

WETT explicitly notes that every inspection level may produce UTI responses.

### Correct use

UTI should be used when an item is within the report framework but the inspector cannot establish its condition within the agreed access level.

Examples may include:

- Concealed component during a Visual inspection.
- Component requiring invasive access during a Technical inspection.
- Flue area blocked from observation.
- Area unsafe or otherwise inaccessible at the time of inspection.

The exact cause of UTI should be documented rather than converted into an assumption.

### Report Wording

> Unable to Inspect — the component/area could not be evaluated within the agreed inspection scope. No compliance conclusion is made for this item.

# 8. What an Inspector Must Not Claim

### Verified rule

WETT certification applies to the trained/certified **individual**, not to:

- The appliance.
- The chimney.
- The installation.
- The house.
- The company.

- “WETT certified fireplace.”

- “WETT inspection report.”
- “Inspected by a WETT-certified professional.”

# 9. Inspection Date and Report Validity

## Classification
**WETT REQUIREMENT / PROCEDURE**

### Verified rule

WETT states that an inspection report documents the conditions observed at the **date and time of inspection**.

After the inspector leaves, the inspector cannot know or control later changes to the system.

Therefore, the report cannot guarantee unchanged future conditions.

### Insurance Acceptance Period

WETT does not establish one universal expiration period for insurer acceptance.

WETT directs customers to the insurer for the insurer’s own acceptance guidelines.

### Operational rule

Never tell a customer:

> “A WETT report is valid for X years”

unless the specific requesting insurer/AHJ has supplied that requirement.

Preferred wording:

> The report records conditions observed on the inspection date. Acceptance timeframes, if any, are determined by the requesting insurer or authority.

# 17. Escalation to a Higher Inspection Level

## Visual → Technical

Escalation may be appropriate when:

- The Visual inspection reveals concern/red flags.
- Hands-on component access is needed.
- A system has been unused for a significant period.
- System components have been replaced/modified.
- More detailed examination is requested by an insurer/AHJ.
- Visual limitations prevent resolution of an important concern.

## Technical → Invasive

Escalation may be appropriate when:

- Concealed components require investigation.
- Critical components remain inaccessible.
- A chimney fire occurred or is suspected.
- A seismic/structural event creates concern.
- Serious fundamental aspects of the installation are suspected to be problematic.
- The investigation requires opening walls/ceilings or invasive chimney work.

## Comprehensive Inspector escalation

Member SITE guidance states that where the inspector determines or strongly suspects a serious problem with fundamental aspects of the installation, a **SITE Comprehensive Inspector** should be suggested.

# 21. Inspection Limitations

A WETT inspection conclusion must remain bounded by:

1. The agreed SITE® inspection level.
2. What was actually visible/accessed.
3. What could be measured.
4. Available appliance/system identification.
5. Available manufacturer documentation.
6. Applicable code/standard information.
7. Conditions present on the inspection date.
8. Inspector qualification/certification scope.

### Core reporting rule

**No access = no fabricated conclusion.**

Where access is not achieved:

- Use UTI where applicable.
- Explain the limitation.
- Recommend the next appropriate step if further investigation is warranted.

# Inspection Application

## A. Pre-Inspection Intake — Common Core

Before attending or beginning the inspection, the inspector should establish:

- Client name/contact information.
- Inspection address.
- Reason for inspection.
- Requested inspection level, if specified.
- Who requested it: homeowner / buyer / seller / insurer / AHJ / other.
- Whether insurer/AHJ supplied special instructions.
- Appliance/system type if known.
- Approximate age if known.
- Whether a certification/data label is visible.
- Whether installation/manual documentation is available.
- Whether the appliance will be cool/not operating for inspection.
- Prior WETT reports.
- Known incidents: smoke spillage, chimney fire, damage, structural event.
- Agreed scope/fees/report-delivery expectations.

---

## B. On-Site — Visual Inspection Core

The inspector should:

1. Confirm agreed inspection scope.
2. Identify the appliance/system as far as evidence allows.
3. Record certification/marking information visible at this level.
4. Inspect readily accessible appliance components.
5. Inspect readily accessible venting components.
6. Measure applicable visible clearances.
7. Operate/open accessible doors, dampers, and ground-level cleanouts as within scope.
8. Inspect chimney from ground.
9. Inspect easily visible flue portions.
10. Record deficiencies/red flags.
11. Record UTI items.
12. Capture required evidence/photos according to current WETT SOP once verified.
13. Escalate if concern requires Technical/Invasive inspection.

---

## C. On-Site — Technical Inspection Core

In addition to the Visual workflow:

1. Use appropriate hand tools/ladders.
2. Access roof/attic where required and safe.
3. Remove/open accessible panels/coverings.
4. Disassemble components such as flue pipes where appropriate.
5. Examine newly exposed areas.
6. Record condition/creosote observations.
7. Identify any areas still inaccessible without invasive work.
8. Document exactly what was opened/accessed.
9. Do not convert inaccessible concealed components into assumed compliance.
10. Escalate to Invasive/Comprehensive inspection where justified.

---

## D. On-Site — Invasive Inspection Core

1. Define precise purpose before invasive work.
2. Obtain client agreement in writing.
3. Confirm any AHJ/permit implications where structural building elements may be compromised.
4. Open only the areas necessary for the defined investigation.
5. Document areas/components exposed.
6. Use specialty equipment where appropriate.
7. Record observations and limitations.
8. Separate inspection from repair/restoration scope.
9. Provide narrative reporting of invasive findings.
10. Clearly communicate required corrective actions before reuse where WETT guidance indicates the system should not be used.

# Reporting Guidance

## Required Reporting Logic

For every report line/item:

### 1. What was the inspection level?
Visual / Technical / Invasive.

### 2. Could the item be inspected at that level?
If no → UTI where applicable.

### 3. What was observed?
Record factual condition.

### 4. What requirement was applied?
Later phases must supply:
- code,
- standard,
- manufacturer instruction,
- or other applicable authority.

### 5. What status is justified?
- Compliant
- Not Compliant
- Not Applicable
- UTI

### 6. Is further action needed?
Possible outcomes:
- no further action noted,
- correction/repair,
- sweeping,
- manufacturer lookup,
- Technical inspection,
- Invasive inspection,
- SITE Comprehensive Inspector,
- AHJ/permit inquiry,
- case-specific evaluation.

---

## Standard UTI Wording

> **Unable to Inspect (UTI):** The identified component/area could not be evaluated within the agreed SITE® inspection scope. No compliance conclusion is made for that component/area. Further access or a higher inspection level may be required if evaluation is necessary.

---

## Standard Escalation Wording

> Conditions observed during this inspection warrant further investigation beyond the present inspection scope. A higher SITE® inspection level and/or an appropriately qualified WETT-certified professional is recommended before a final conclusion is made regarding the affected area.

---

## Standard Point-in-Time Wording

> This report documents conditions observed at the date and time of inspection. It does not warrant that the installation will remain unchanged after the inspection.

---

## Standard “Not a Certificate” Wording

> This document is a WETT inspection report completed by a WETT-certified individual. WETT certification applies to the inspector; WETT does not certify the appliance, installation, or company.

---

# Exceptions / Edge Cases

## 1. Client asks for “WETT certificate”

Correct the terminology professionally. Do not argue with the customer.

Use:

> We can provide a WETT inspection report completed by a WETT-certified inspector. WETT certifies individuals, not fireplaces or installations.

---

## 2. Insurer says “Level 1 WETT”

Treat this as a request for a Visual inspection unless the insurer’s actual form/instructions say otherwise.

Do not assume that every insurer uses identical wording or requirements.

---

## 3. Serious concern during Visual inspection

Do not stretch the Visual inspection into an unsupported conclusion.

Document:
- observed concern,
- limitation,
- UTI where applicable,
- recommended Technical/Invasive escalation.

---

## 4. Serious concern during Technical inspection

If concealed components prevent resolution, an Invasive inspection may be required.

Where a serious fundamental problem is determined or strongly suspected, current member SITE guidance indicates that a SITE Comprehensive Inspector should be suggested.

---

## 5. Appliance has no readable label

Do not invent manufacturer/model/listing data.

Record what is actually visible.

**MANUFACTURER LOOKUP REQUIRED** or **NOT VERIFIED** until supporting evidence is obtained.

---

## 6. Customer asks whether report “expires”

Explain that the report is a point-in-time record. The insurer/AHJ determines its own acceptance period.

---

## 7. Customer asks inspector to “just pass it”

There is no defensible “pass it anyway” path.

The report must reflect actual observed findings and access limitations.

WETT Code of Ethics prohibits knowingly false or misleading reports.
