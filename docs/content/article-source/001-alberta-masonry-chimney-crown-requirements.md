# Alberta Masonry Chimney Crown Requirements: What Homeowners Should Know

## Direct Answer

For masonry and concrete chimneys covered by Part 9 of Alberta's current building code, the top of the chimney must have a **waterproof cap** made of reinforced concrete, masonry, or metal.

Under **National Building Code – 2023 Alberta Edition, Division B, Article 9.21.4.6**, that cap must:

- be waterproof;
- slope away from the chimney lining so water can drain;
- include a drip located at least **25 mm from the chimney wall**;
- use a bond break and seal where a cast-in-place concrete cap meets the chimney liner; and
- where a jointed precast concrete or masonry cap is used, have flashing underneath extending from the liner to the drip edge.

In everyday chimney-service language, homeowners often call the concrete slab at the top the **chimney crown**, while the metal cover over the flue is often called the **chimney cap**. The Alberta code article itself uses the term **chimney cap** for the waterproof top protection over the masonry. This distinction matters when discussing what the code actually requires.

## Why This Matters

A masonry chimney is exposed to rain, snow, melting ice, and repeated freeze/thaw cycles.

The top surface is one of the most exposed parts of the chimney. If water is allowed to enter the masonry from above, it can contribute to deterioration over time. A properly constructed crown/cap is intended to shed water away from the chimney rather than allowing it to soak into the brick and mortar below.

For Alberta homeowners, this is especially important because exterior masonry can experience repeated freezing and thawing during the year.

A crown that looks flat, cracked, poorly sealed around the flue liner, or flush with the outside face of the brick may deserve closer inspection. Appearance alone does not establish a code violation, but these conditions can indicate that the chimney is not managing water as intended.

## Official Requirements / Guidance

### CODE REQUIREMENT — NBC(AE) 2023 Article 9.21.4.6

The current code in force in Alberta is the **National Building Code – 2023 Alberta Edition**.

For masonry and concrete chimneys within the scope of Section 9.21, Article 9.21.4.6 requires the following:

1. The top of the chimney must have a waterproof cap of reinforced concrete, masonry, or metal.
2. The cap must slope from the lining.
3. The cap must provide a drip at least 25 mm from the chimney wall.
4. A cast-in-place concrete cap must be separated from the chimney liner by a bond break and sealed at that location.
5. A jointed precast concrete or masonry cap requires flashing beneath the cap from the liner to the drip edge.

### CODE REQUIREMENT — Adjacent flashing

Article **9.21.4.10** also requires junctions with adjacent materials to be adequately flashed to shed water.

This is a separate issue from the crown itself, but both details are part of the chimney's overall water-management system.

### OFFICIAL EXPLANATION — Why the cap matters

The NRC's Illustrated User's Guide for Part 9 explains the intent behind this requirement: rain entering the top of masonry can make chimney masonry more vulnerable to freeze/thaw damage.

The guide describes common cap construction as concrete extending beyond the masonry with a drip edge, and explains that the cap must slope so water can run off instead of remaining on top of the chimney.

The guide is explanatory material. It helps interpret the requirement, but the enforceable wording comes from the applicable code.

## What This Means for the Homeowner

A chimney crown should not be thought of as decorative mortar placed over the top course of brick.

Its job is water management.

From a homeowner's point of view, the important questions are:

- Does the top surface shed water away from the flue?
- Is there a projecting edge that directs water away from the chimney wall?
- Is the crown/cap visibly cracked or separating?
- Is there an open or deteriorated joint where the flue liner passes through the crown?
- Is water staining or masonry deterioration visible below the top of the chimney?
- Does the chimney have other water-entry points, such as failed flashing or damaged masonry?

A chimney can have a metal rain cap over the flue and still have a deficient masonry crown below it. These are different components serving different functions.

## Phoenix Field Knowledge

Phoenix field inspections focus first on two visible conditions at the top of the chimney:

- **deep cracking in the crown**, especially cracks that appear to extend through the crown rather than remaining superficial; and
- the condition and geometry of the **drip edge**, because the crown must direct water away from the masonry below.

In Phoenix field experience, one of the more serious conditions encountered is **large sections of the crown breaking apart or deteriorating**.

Phoenix generally treats a crown as a repair candidate when the damage remains localized and the cracking or deterioration has **not progressed into the brickwork below**.

A **full crown rebuild** becomes more appropriate when there is major breakage, when damaged areas extend into or affect the masonry below, or when the concrete itself has become severely deteriorated or is crumbling.

Phoenix also encounters attempts to stop crown leaks using surface-applied silicone. Silicone may temporarily cover a crack, but Phoenix does not treat it as a substitute for rebuilding a failed crown. Alberta winter conditions and repeated freeze/thaw exposure can make superficial sealant-only repairs unreliable when the underlying crown is already structurally deteriorated.

These observations are Phoenix field experience and should not be represented as code requirements. Final repair scope depends on the actual condition of the crown, masonry, flue interface, and surrounding water-management details.

## Safe Homeowner Observations

A homeowner can often notice warning signs from the ground or from a safe indoor location without climbing onto the roof.

Possible observations include:

- pieces of mortar or masonry found on the roof or ground;
- obvious cracking visible in photographs taken safely from a distance;
- white staining or efflorescence on exterior masonry;
- interior moisture staining near the chimney;
- visibly deteriorated upper brickwork;
- a missing or damaged metal rain cap.

Homeowners should not climb onto steep or icy roofs to inspect a chimney closely.

Do not chip, grind, drill, remove masonry, remove the flue cap, or attempt structural crown repairs without appropriate access, equipment, and knowledge.

## When Qualified Service Is Appropriate

A closer chimney inspection is appropriate when:

- the crown is visibly cracked or deteriorated;
- water is entering near the chimney;
- upper masonry is spalling or breaking down;
- a previous crown repair is failing;
- the flue-to-crown joint is open or deteriorated;
- the chimney has no clear water-shedding crown detail;
- flashing or masonry defects may be contributing to the same leak;
- the homeowner is unsure whether the existing construction matches the applicable requirements.

The objective should be to identify the actual water-entry path and the condition of the chimney before choosing a repair.

A small surface crack and a structurally failed crown are not the same repair problem.

## FAQ

### Is a chimney crown required in Alberta?

For masonry and concrete chimneys within the scope of NBC(AE) Section 9.21, the top of the chimney is required to have a waterproof cap of reinforced concrete, masonry, or metal.

### How far should a chimney crown overhang?

The code wording requires a **drip not less than 25 mm from the chimney wall**. The code is describing the required drip location, not simply giving a generic decorative overhang dimension.

### Does the chimney crown need to slope?

Yes. Article 9.21.4.6 requires the cap to slope from the lining so that water is directed away.

### Can the crown be made from mortar?

Do not reduce the code requirement to “mortar is allowed” or “mortar is prohibited” without evaluating the actual assembly. The code specifies a waterproof cap of reinforced concrete, masonry, or metal and additional detailing requirements. A thin mortar wash should not automatically be represented as equivalent to a properly detailed crown.

### Is the metal cover above the flue the same thing as the chimney crown?

No in normal service terminology. The concrete or masonry water-shedding top is usually called the crown, while the metal cover over the flue is commonly called the chimney cap or rain cap.

However, the building code itself uses **chimney cap** in Article 9.21.4.6 when referring to the waterproof top protection of the masonry chimney. The article should make this terminology clear to avoid confusion.

### Does a cracked crown automatically mean the chimney must be rebuilt?

No. The repair depends on the extent of the cracking, water penetration, existing crown construction, masonry condition, liner interface, and whether the existing detail can be repaired properly.

### Can a homeowner inspect the crown from the roof?

A homeowner may be able to observe the chimney safely from the ground or through photographs, but roof access introduces fall hazards. Detailed inspection and repair should be performed with appropriate access and safety equipment.
